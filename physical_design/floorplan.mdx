# Floorplanning

> Floorplanning is the process of arranging circuit blocks on a chip to meet performance, area, power, and routability goals. This page gives a practical overview and checklist for an efficient floorplanning flow.

## Objectives

- Place major functional blocks to minimize interconnect length and congestion.
- Respect timing and power budgets by controlling block proximity and orientation.
- Provide enough whitespace for routing and timing optimizations.

## Key Concepts

- **Macro**: Large pre-designed block (e.g., memory, I/O) placed early in the flow.
- **Standard-cell area**: Regions filled with standard cells; keep contiguous for routability.
- **Fence regions**: Constrain where certain blocks may be placed to reduce congestion.
- **Pin access**: Ensure pins on macros are reachable for routing engines.

## Practical Floorplanning Steps

1. Identify macros and large hard-IP blocks and fix their aspect ratios.
2. Create an initial placement sketch (logical-to-physical mapping) grouping related modules.
3. Reserve space for power/clock distribution and I/O pads.
4. Iterate: place macros → run congestion/timing analysis → adjust fences/regions.
5. Finalize placement with legalization and a timing-driven detailed placement step.

## Tips

- Start with a coarse-grained plan: cluster modules by hierarchy and signal density.
- Use whitespace strategically to give the router flexibility; avoid tightly packed islands.
- Keep timing-critical paths short by colocating producer/consumer blocks.
- Re-run congestion and timing checks after each significant placement change.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Macros placed and oriented
- [ ] Power/ground channels reserved
- [ ] Timing hotspots identified
- [ ] Routing congestion within limits
- [ ] Pin accessibility verified
